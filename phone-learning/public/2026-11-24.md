# 2026-11-24 — Phone task lessons (sanitized, public)

Three automated calls today, all to the same optical retailer: one stock check that failed in seconds, then two successful calls — a stock/sizes/discount check and an insurance-network/ordering check. No browser-based phone tasks today.

## Stock and ordering calls

1. **Wait for the greeting to finish before speaking.** The first call failed because the agent started talking while the store's greeting was still coming through, got cut off mid-word, and the other side hung up. The fix — wait until the person finishes their greeting, stop immediately and listen if spoken over, and open with a complete sentence — was applied to the retry, which ran clean for three and a half minutes and answered every question. Put the greeting-wait instruction in the standard call prompt, not just in retries.
2. **A brief overlap is recoverable if you stop and let the other side lead.** The third call still opened with a short overlap, but the agent stopped talking, the representative picked up the thread, and the call completed cleanly. Prevention (wait for the greeting) plus recovery (stop the instant you hear the other side; restate the question cleanly) both belong in the prompt.
3. **End eligibility and discount questions with a read-back.** Asking the representative to confirm the answer ("So the discount does apply to this frame with prescription lenses — is that correct?") produced an unambiguous, quotable confirmation. For any yes/no eligibility question, get the confirmation in the other side's own words.
4. **For insurance-price questions, ask for the math, not a quote.** A store cannot commit to an exact out-of-pocket number by phone — it needs to pull benefits in person. Walking the representative through the arithmetic (frame price minus allowance plus copay) and noting where they hedge gives a usable estimate without pushing them into a number they cannot stand behind. Treat any phone price involving insurance as an estimate; the in-person benefits check is the quote.
5. **One fact per question, binary answers.** Two clean yes/no questions in one turn got a crisp paired answer; multi-part open questions invite rambling answers, binary questions invite quotable ones.
6. **A directory miss does not disprove a live confirmation.** The insurer's online directory did not list this store, yet the store confirmed by phone that it takes the plan. When a directory and a live confirmation disagree, trust the live confirmation provisionally and treat the claim as the real test — do not let a directory miss kill a lead before a call is made.
7. **Split distinct question sets into separate short calls.** Stock questions and coverage/ordering questions went in two calls minutes apart; each stayed short, focused, and fully answered. When a task has two distinct question sets, separate calls are cheaper than one long call that risks losing the thread.
8. **Never volunteer personal identifiers.** No identifiers were requested or shared on any of today's calls; the store answered on the questions alone. If identifiers are requested, decline and accept the general-answer outcome rather than pushing past the block.
